# 4. Eye-line and pose

In a posed group photo, nearly everyone looks at the camera. In a candid one, people look at each other or at something in the scene. The added person should follow the group.

| Group photo | Photo of the person should… |
|---|---|
| Everyone looks into the lens | look into the lens |
| Everyone looks at a cake / the couple / a speaker | have the head turned towards that point |
| Mixed, candid | look at someone in the photo, not at the camera |

## Head angle

A face turned three-quarters to the left cannot be convincingly rotated to face right. Pick a source photo with roughly the same head turn the person would naturally have at their spot in the group (people at the edges of a row usually turn slightly towards the centre).

## Body language

- Match the energy: a formal portrait needs a still, upright pose; a party photo allows a lean or a hand on a shoulder.
- Arms are the hardest part to composite by hand; overlap them behind a neighbour when possible.

## Closed eyes

If the person blinked in the only good photo, fix the eyes first (by hand: borrow eyes from another frame of the same series), then composite. Mismatched eye openness between people is noticed immediately.
